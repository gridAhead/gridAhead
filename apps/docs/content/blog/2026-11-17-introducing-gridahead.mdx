**Berlin, November 17, 2026** — Power grids are one of the most critical bottlenecks in Germany’s energy transition. Since the end of October, Germany’s larger distribution system operators have been publishing a second round of their grid expansion plans with a total of over 33,000 measures. Unfortunately, the currently available data are difficult to use for research, planning, investment decisions, and public policy and it often does not adhere to open-data standards. To address this challenge, the Reiner Lemoine Institute (RLI) and Goal100 have launched gridAhead, an open-source and open-data community project that will transform published grid expansion plans into accessible, georeferenced datasets. The project is online at [www.gridahead.org](https://www.gridahead.org).

## Grid expansion data: essential but difficult to use

Whether new wind and solar farms, battery storage systems, heat pumps, or electric vehicle charging infrastructure can be connected to the power grid depends largely on how quickly distribution grids are reinforced and expanded. German distribution system operators serving 100,000 customers or more are legally required to publish grid expansion plans regularly. The first round in 2024 came as PDF only. The second round of this information still remains difficult to use. 80 out of 85 network operators now deliver their measures, together over 33,000 of which about 20,000 are ongoing or planned. Basic information on the geographical location remains generally unclear.

## Turning plans into open, machine-readable data

The 2026 round also brought some improvements: most operators now publish their measures as spreadsheets in a common format. But the measures still have no coordinates, the grid maps are still only available as images in PDF reports, and most files are not released under open licences. With gridAhead, RLI and Goal100 aim to convert grid expansion plans into openly available geospatial datasets. The project focuses on four core activities:

1. **Geocode planned measures.** The project locates announced measures of both the 2024 and 2026 expansion plans. This shows project developers where and when the grid near a site will be reinforced.
2. **Georeference grid maps.** Wherever possible, the project digitizes the grid maps in the plans and enriches them with other public data. The aim is to publish open data on the current and planned medium-voltage grid in Germany. Grid studies can then use topologies based on real grid maps instead of the synthetic or model grids mostly used today.
3. **Make it accessible.** The project publishes its results under open licences whenever possible, so others can keep working with them, instead of starting from the original documents again. Data can be reused without legal uncertainty, and researchers can cite one versioned dataset instead of extracting the same PDFs again.
4. **Build a community.** The project aims to build an open community that uses, reviews and further develops data and methods. Many users improve data and methods faster than any single team could.

## Building a new community around grid expansion plans

Downstream users of German grid expansion plans are a diverse group of actors, spanning across private and public sectors, as well as scientific institutions. What they all have in common is the need for reliable and accurate data. gridAhead is about bringing together these actors and consolidating efforts. Interested users of this data are invited to get in touch with the project team. The partners emphasize that making grid expansion plans more accessible does not conflict with the protection of critical infrastructure. The project focuses on information that is already published by distribution system operators but remains difficult to analyse and reuse for planning, research or policy making.

## Background

Under Sections [14d](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/enwg_2005/__14d.html) and [14e](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/enwg_2005/__14e.html) of the German Energy Industry Act (EnWG), distribution system operators with 100,000 or more connected customers, as well as smaller operators subject to significant curtailment of wind or solar power, must draw up a grid expansion plan every two years and publish it on the distribution system operators’ joint online platform, [VNBdigital.de](https://www.vnbdigital.de/). The plans say where the network is expected to grow, which measures are foreseen, and on what timetable. That information bears on private and public investment, on research, and on political discourse about where the energy transition can actually proceed, and where it cannot. The RLI and a broad alliance from research, NGO, industry and associations have already called for action in an [open letter](https://reiner-lemoine-institut.de/offener-brief-an-bnetza-netzdaten-besser-nutzbar-machen/) to the German Federal Network Agency (BNetzA) in 2024. The new project is the next step to improve the use of German grid expansion data. gridAhead will document the method and results on [www.gridahead.org](https://www.gridahead.org) and in the public repository [github.com/gridahead/gridahead](https://github.com/gridahead/gridahead).

## About the Reiner Lemoine Institute

The Reiner Lemoine Institute (RLI) is an independent, non-profit research institute that has been committed to a future with 100 percent renewable energy since 2010. In the three research units Transformation of Energy Systems, Mobility with Renewable Energy and Off-Grid Systems, RLI scientists work in an application-oriented and scientific manner for the energy and transport transition in Germany and internationally. Since its foundation, the open-source models developed at the institute have become firmly established in energy system modeling. RLI's mobility and electrification concepts are implemented by companies and the public sector worldwide. [www.reiner-lemoine-institut.de](https://www.reiner-lemoine-institut.de/)

## About Goal100

Goal100 is a think-and-do tank that combines non-profit data services with services for project developers and investors. Goal100 is best known for its public wind monitor, which collects permit data on wind projects, and offers a free energy transition web GIS at [www.goal100.studio](https://www.goal100.studio). Goal100's initiative for gridAhead is jointly funded by [Google.org](https://www.google.org/) and the [Veolia Foundation](https://www.stiftung.veolia.de/) and hosted by [ProjectTogether gGmbH](https://projecttogether.org/).
